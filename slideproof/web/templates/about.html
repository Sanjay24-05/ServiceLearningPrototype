How Slide Proof works

Slide Proof checks how your slides will look to a sighted audience and tells you, in words, what a sighted person would notice. It can also fix some problems for you, on a copy.

What "Probably intentional" means

Some things that look like mistakes are deliberate design: a logo that bleeds off a corner, a band that runs along an edge, a background shape behind everything. Slide Proof has a small model that runs on this computer and learned from example decks which patterns usually look deliberate. When it is fairly sure, the finding goes under "Probably intentional" with the reasons. Nothing is hidden: it is still listed and counted, and its fix is available but not ticked for you. The model learned from synthetic example decks, so it is a guess; check those findings if you are unsure.

How accurate the model is

How fixes work

Fixes are only applied to a copy of your file, never the original. After fixing, Slide Proof checks the copy again and undoes any fix that did not work or caused a new problem.

Your privacy

Your file stays on this computer. Nothing is uploaded to the internet, and files from earlier sessions are deleted each time Slide Proof starts.
